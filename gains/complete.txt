## 2. Grammar
### 2.1. Words
Here we'll describe the morphology of the words
#### 2.1.1. Cases
-s (from si): nominative, it shows who does what;
-f (from fe): accusative, who gets directly the action
-i (from ints): dative, shows the target of an action
-k (from krja): genitive, we restrict something that is ours
-p: locative (from pul), where something occurs
-m: instrumental (from mok), what is used to do something
#### 2.1.2. Plural
use -ǐ for plural after consonants, or -oǐ after consonants (the idea to separate vowel and consonant is inspired on tenksz, from Seatlll8292 in r/conlangs, and the "-j" idea came from Esperanto)
### Verbs
now, we'll describe how to conjugate verbs
#### 2.1.3. Tenses
for indicate tense, where in time action is located, we use:
- -t: for past  
- -o for present  
- -i for future  
- -u for hypothetic (a scenario out of time, or non-factual)  
Gains doesn't have a distinction like "I have eaten" vs "I ate/ "I eat" vs "I am eating".
#### 2.1.4. Volition
We need to specify **our intention** on the verbs — that's called volition:
-a: desire
(nothing): habit/instinct
-e: for needing/forcing
-ü: accident
### 2.2. Some extra details
There is no "to be" verb, so, you just do "word word" or "word adjective" for example.
#### 2.2.1 Pragmatic
We use elements order to indicate phrase type. First, let's define some elements, using the case suffixes:  
S (Subject) = S (nominative)
O (Objects) = K*PFIM (genitive, locative, accusative, dative, instrumental)
V (Verb) = Verb
Note: K* indicates that can appear in any position.
Note 2: That's flexible, with restrictions only for making questions and affirmations
Now, we can indicate how to ask and how to affirm.
   1. SOV = affirmation  
   2. OVS = questions  
## 3. Examples
Let's see various examples:
tis huk! (It's mine!!)
χotinf umaf! (The food is good!)
xotinf kûgf! (The food is terrible!)
hus mei sioa (I do it for you)
hus χotinf fetü (I've got food)
hus krap ĝaf pult! (I've seen she/he in forest!)
tif jumo mes? (do you want it?)
hus ǔafrikojf umājoa (I like the people)
hus ifīp tif sioe (I do it tomorrow)
hus mef ümaoa, ja ĝaf ǖmaoa (I want to hurt you, and have sex with they)
hus huf hui huk xotoa (I eat myself with my me)
ǔafrikojs kûgs (people are bad)
kûgs ǔafrikojs (bad people)
hus alexandrof ka umājoü (I don't like Alejandro)
"ga"s "speak"s, "in"s "thing"s, ja "s"s "nominative"s,
lux "gains"s Engliśp "the language"s! ("ga" is "speak", "in" is "thing", and "s" is nominative, so/then "gains" is "the language" in English!)
and then, we can have a simple dialog:
hus umaf xotinf jumoa (I want good food!)
ja xotins umas (And the food is good!)
ûmmmm... ka ǔapuloa... tis mek, ka?...(hummmm... I don't know... from you/it's from you, is not?)
